PID (Process ID)

A PID is like a coat-check ticket NUMBER printed on a public sign board — anyone walking past can read it, remember it, and mention it to someone else. Knowing the number tells you nothing about whether you're allowed to actually claim the coat.

A PID is a plain, small integer the kernel assigns to a process when it's created — globally visible, easily obtained (any process can enumerate every PID on the system via a Toolhelp32 snapshot, exactly as Module 4's own count_threads() helper does), and carrying zero access rights of its own.

Knowing a process's PID lets you ATTEMPT to interact with it — call OpenProcess(pid, ...), for instance — but whether that attempt succeeds is decided entirely by a completely separate mechanism (a handle, checked against your own token). A PID is an identity label, not a key.

Confused with Handle constantly — see the connections hub for the exact, common wrong statement this leads to.
