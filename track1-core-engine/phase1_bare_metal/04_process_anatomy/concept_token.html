Token (Access Token)

A token is your entire employee ID badge — your name, your department, every door group you belong to, and a printed clearance LEVEL — the complete credential a security guard actually inspects before deciding whether today's badge opens today's door.

A token is the kernel object representing WHO a process is allowed to act as: its user identity, its group memberships, its specific granted/denied privileges, and (central to Module 9's entire module) its integrity level. Every single access check the kernel performs — including the ones deciding whether a handle request succeeds — consults the calling process's token.

Module 9 proved a token's most important practical property directly: a process's token can be DUPLICATED and DELIBERATELY NARROWED (lowering its integrity level) before spawning a child — and that narrowed child, even though it's still running as the exact same underlying user account, genuinely has fewer real rights than its parent.

A token doesn't identify a SPECIFIC resource — it identifies WHO is asking. The thing it's compared AGAINST, to decide integrity-level outcomes, is built from SIDs. See the connections hub.
