SameSite (Cookie Attribute)

SOP and CORS are both about who's allowed to OPEN mail. SameSite is a completely different question: whether your signature (a cookie) gets attached to a letter someone ELSE wrote and mailed on your behalf, without you ever seeing it.

SameSite is a per-cookie attribute (Strict, Lax, or None) controlling whether that cookie rides along on a cross-site request. Module 29 proved its real, precise rules: Lax specifically requires BOTH a top-level navigation AND a safe method — losing either condition withholds the cookie, even cross-site.

Module 32 showed exactly why this matters independent of SOP/CORS: a CSRF attack never needs to READ any response at all — it only needs the victim's cookie to ride along on a request the attacker's page triggers, which is precisely what SameSite governs and neither SOP nor CORS ever touches.

Frequently confused with SOP's "same-origin" concept — SameSite uses the LOOSER "same-site" (registrable domain) comparison instead. See the connections hub for the exact wrong statement this produces.
