HSTS (HTTP Strict Transport Security)

Every other term on this page assumes the envelope genuinely reached the right building through a real, private courier. HSTS is the rule that makes sure the courier NEVER takes the old, unlocked delivery route again, once you've told it not to — it operates one full layer below all four other policies, before any of their questions are even relevant.

HSTS is a browser-enforced policy, learned from a Strict-Transport-Security response header, that rewrites future http:// requests to a host into https:// BEFORE any network request is made at all. Module 31 proved the ordering directly: the rewrite happens client-side, pre-flight — never as a server-side redirect an attacker could still intercept the first plaintext request before.

HSTS is checked FIRST, before any of the other four policies on this page even become relevant — SOP, CORS, SameSite, and CSP all implicitly assume the connection itself wasn't already silently downgraded.

Often lumped in with generic "HTTPS stuff" — HSTS is specifically about preventing DOWNGRADE, not about encryption itself (that's TLS's job, Module 15). See the connections hub for the complete five-policy picture.
