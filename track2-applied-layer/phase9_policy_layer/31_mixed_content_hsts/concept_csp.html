CSP (Content Security Policy)

Every other term on this page governs mail crossing BETWEEN addresses. CSP is different: it's a standing rule about what's allowed to happen INSIDE your own house, even with things that arrived through your own front door.

CSP is a server-declared, browser-enforced allowlist governing which SCRIPTS (and other resources) a page is even allowed to execute — checked BEFORE execution, regardless of whether that script is same-origin or cross-origin, injected or intended. Module 30 proved the ordering directly: blocked scripts never reach an "executed" list at all.

Module 32 showed CSP's real, primary value: it's a SECOND, independent layer against XSS, neutralizing an already-successful injection's ability to run — not a cross-origin policy at all, and not a fix for the underlying injection bug.

Easy to mistake for a cross-origin policy like SOP or CORS — CSP governs EXECUTION of content already on the page, not cross-origin reading. See the connections hub.
